# Pig's Tails

## by Gill Ord

Using information compression techniques, Ord produces an analogue to her painters' studio practice in which a digitally captured video frame is slowly un-picked through time.

**Technical Director:** Peter Moore _- e-2_

**Produced by:** e-2

**Viewing requirements:** A modern browser with JavaScript enabled

## How it works

A blurred video still of a market stall is slowly covered with flat patches of colour, then the photograph goes and the patches are taken away, shown again one by one, and pieces of the photograph come back, before it starts again (1003 frames at 10 frames a second, about 100 seconds).

- **Patches under the pointer.** While the photograph shows, moving the pointer over it shows patches before they are laid down. Once a patch is laid down, it vanishes under the pointer to show the photograph again, and comes back while the mouse button is pressed.
- **The yellow dots below are controls.** Under the pointer each shows what it does: pan left, pan up, zoom out, home, zoom in, pan down, pan right. Originally they sent commands from one Flash movie, through the page's JavaScript, to the Flash plugin's `Zoom` and `Pan` methods on the picture in the other frame. Ruffle doesn't provide those methods, so the controls do nothing there.

## The port

`index.html` plays the piece without Flash, as SVG: every shape and button of the original at its original place, shown at exactly its original frames by CSS animations, with the buttons' original states. The controls zoom and pan the picture as the plugin did. It's generated by `_scripts/build.py` from `map.swf` and `controls.swf`, using [JPEXS FFDec](https://github.com/jindrapetrik/jpexs-decompiler), which also writes `photo.jpg`, the movie's own JPEG:

```sh
python3 _scripts/build.py --ffdec /path/to/ffdec-cli.jar
```
